# Monetization and cost assumptions
A proposal, not observed revenue, margin or willingness to pay.

## Initial offer
Free: daily lesson, answer explanations, due reviews and basic progress.
Plus: full bank access, custom/timed quizzes, full mocks and bounded contextual AI help.
Preview: one-time 14 days, starts only after explicit acceptance, no card and no automatic renewal. Free access returns at expiry. Do not auto-start the preview at signup.

Illustrative test prices: USD 12.99 monthly or 79.99 annually. In the native app use store-localized prices and total billing period. Annual is 79.99 per year, not a misleading monthly charge. Paid subscriptions renew until cancelled through the platform. Taxes, refunds, receipt lifecycle and customer support must be implemented.

For a finite certification goal, also test a nonrenewing 60/90-day exam pack against subscription. A package may better align with graduation, but actual demand is unknown.

## Unit economics worksheet
Monthly recognized gross revenue per annual subscriber at 79.99/year = 6.67 before platform deductions and taxes. This is different from annual cash receipts.
Contribution margin = (recognized revenue − store/payment fees − variable serving/AI/support costs − refunds allocation) / recognized revenue.
Operating profit also includes content review, staff, acquisition, tools and overhead. Competitor private-company profit margins cannot be inferred from subscription prices.

Example sensitivity, using a purely illustrative 15% platform fee and 1.00 variable cost per active paid user-month:
| Plan | Recognized monthly revenue | Fee assumption | Variable cost | Contribution / margin |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Monthly | 12.99 | 1.95 | 1.00 | 10.04 / 77.3% |
| Annual | 6.67 | 1.00 | 1.00 | 4.67 / 70.0% |

At a 30% hypothetical fee, the same examples become about 62.3% and 55.0%. These are arithmetic scenarios, not current store eligibility advice or forecasts. Actual fee terms must be checked at implementation.

The 1.00 variable cost is a placeholder. Build a measured model from model tokens, calls per learner, storage, API/database, support minutes and refund frequency. Do not use competitor company gross margins as our app profit forecast.

## Cost control and trust
Prefer reviewed static rationales. Call AI only on explicit help requests, with a configurable daily budget and a static fallback. Cache source-based concept explanations without storing private user reasoning in a shared cache. Do not create unlimited voice at a fixed low price before measuring cost.

Human content creation, ongoing exam-guide changes and support are central costs, even if AI inference is cheap. Reserve a budget for review and invalidate affected practice when a question is wrong.
